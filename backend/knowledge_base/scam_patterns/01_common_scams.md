# Common Financial Scams Targeting Indians — Recognition and Prevention

## 1. Lottery and Prize Scams
**How it works:** You receive a call, SMS, or WhatsApp message claiming you have won a lottery, prize, or lucky draw (often using brand names like KBC — Kaun Banega Crorepati, RBI Prize, or a foreign lottery). You are asked to pay a "processing fee," "tax," or "customs duty" to claim your prize.

**Red flags:**
- You never entered any contest
- They ask for money upfront to release winnings
- They ask for your Aadhaar, PAN, or bank account number
- The contact number is a regular mobile number, not an official landline

**Reality:** Legitimate lotteries never ask winners to pay fees upfront. KBC winner selection is done through a separate documented process — not random calls. No real prize requires advance payment.

**What to do:** Hang up immediately. Block the number. Report to cybercrime portal: www.cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.

## 2. Fake KYC Update Scams
**How it works:** You receive a call or SMS claiming your bank account, Aadhaar, or UPI will be blocked unless you update your KYC immediately. You are directed to click a link, download an app (often a remote access app like AnyDesk or TeamViewer), or share OTP.

**Red flags:**
- Urgency and threat of account blocking
- Asking for OTP, CVV, or full card number
- Link does not go to official bank/NPCI website
- Asking you to download an unfamiliar app

**Reality:** Banks and NPCI never ask for OTP, password, or CVV over phone or SMS. KYC updates happen at bank branches or through official bank apps only.

**What to do:** Never share OTP with anyone. Disconnect the call. Contact your bank's official helpline.

## 3. Fake Loan App Scams
**How it works:** Predatory loan apps promise instant loans with no documentation. They access your phone contacts and photos during app installation. If you take a loan, they charge extreme interest rates (sometimes 100–500% annualized). If you miss a payment, they threaten to send morphed photos or fake debt messages to your contacts.

**Red flags:**
- App not listed on RBI's registered NBFC list
- Requests access to your contacts, camera, and storage during installation
- No physical address or grievance officer listed
- Interest rates not disclosed clearly before disbursal

**Reality:** Only RBI-registered banks and NBFCs can legally offer loans. Any app offering loans must be backed by a registered entity. RBI has banned multiple such predatory apps.

**What to do:** Check RBI's registered NBFC list at rbi.org.in before borrowing. Never install apps that demand unnecessary permissions. Report abusive apps to cybercrime.gov.in.

## 4. Investment Doubling / Ponzi Schemes
**How it works:** You are promised extraordinary returns — "double your money in 90 days," "guaranteed 3% per month," or "government-backed scheme with 40% annual return." Early investors are paid using money from newer investors, creating the illusion of a real investment. The scheme collapses when new investor money runs out.

**Red flags:**
- Guaranteed or unusually high returns (legitimate equity investments average 10–12% annually over long periods)
- Pressure to recruit others
- No clear explanation of how returns are generated
- Not registered with SEBI

**Reality:** No legitimate investment guarantees high fixed returns. Even government bonds give 7–8% maximum. Any promise above that is a scam or extremely high-risk.

**What to do:** Verify with SEBI's investor portal (scores.sebi.gov.in). Never invest based on WhatsApp forwards or social media tips.

## 5. Job Offer / Work-from-Home Scams
**How it works:** A fake recruiter offers a high-paying work-from-home job requiring you to pay a registration fee, training fee, or security deposit. Some schemes involve "task-based" earning where you are asked to like YouTube videos, rate products, or do data entry — and must first deposit money to "unlock" earnings.

**Red flags:**
- Asking for money before you start working
- No company address, GST number, or verifiable online presence
- Payment via UPI to a personal account, not a company account
- Job offer without any interview

**Reality:** Legitimate employers never ask candidates to pay money. "Pay to earn" task platforms are almost always scams where you lose your deposit.

---

## 6. OTP and UPI Fraud
**How it works:** A fraudster calls pretending to be a bank official, asks you to share an OTP "to verify your account," or sends a collect request on UPI with a note like "receive ₹5,000." Approving a UPI collect request does not receive money — it sends money from your account.

**Red flags:**
- Any request to share OTP with anyone
- UPI collect request from unknown person
- Caller claims to be from RBI, bank, or government

**Reality:** OTP is your digital signature. Sharing it is equivalent to handing someone your debit card and PIN. RBI and banks never ask for OTP.

**Helpline:** National Cybercrime Helpline: **1930**
Online reporting: **www.cybercrime.gov.in**
