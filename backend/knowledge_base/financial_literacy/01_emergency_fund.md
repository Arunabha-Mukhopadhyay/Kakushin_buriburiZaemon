# Emergency Fund — What It Is and Why Every Indian Needs One

## What is an Emergency Fund?
An emergency fund is money kept aside specifically for unexpected financial shocks — job loss, medical emergency, urgent home repair, or any crisis that demands immediate cash. It is not for planned expenses like a vacation or a gadget upgrade.

## How Much Should You Save?
The standard recommendation for India:
- **Salaried employees:** 3 to 6 months of total monthly expenses
- **Gig workers, freelancers, self-employed:** 6 to 12 months of total monthly expenses
- **Single-income households with dependents:** Minimum 6 months

### Example
If your monthly expenses (rent, food, transport, EMIs, utilities) are ₹20,000:
- Salaried target: ₹60,000 to ₹1,20,000
- Gig worker target: ₹1,20,000 to ₹2,40,000

## Where to Keep Your Emergency Fund
The fund must be **liquid** (accessible within 24 hours) and **separate** from your regular bank account so you do not accidentally spend it.

**Best options in India:**
1. **High-interest Savings Account** — Zero-lock-in, instant access. Look for accounts offering 4–7% interest (some small finance banks like ESAF, AU Small Finance Bank offer 7%+).
2. **Liquid Mutual Funds** — Slightly higher return than savings accounts (~6–7%), redeemable within 1 business day. Examples: HDFC Liquid Fund, SBI Liquid Fund.
3. **Sweep-in Fixed Deposit (Auto-sweep FD)** — Excess money in savings is auto-converted to FD at higher interest; swept back when needed. Offered by most major banks.

**Avoid keeping emergency funds in:**
- Equity mutual funds or stocks (value can drop exactly when you need the money most)
- Long-term FDs with lock-in periods
- PPF (has lock-in; only partial withdrawal allowed after 7 years)

## How to Build It If You Have No Savings
Start small and be consistent:
1. Open a separate zero-balance savings account (Jan Dhan account works)
2. Set up an automatic transfer of even ₹500–₹1,000 per month on salary day
3. Add any windfall (bonus, tax refund, gift money) directly to this fund
4. Do not break it for non-emergencies — label it clearly in your mind as "off-limits"

## Signs You Are Ready to Invest Further
Only start SIPs in equity mutual funds or other higher-risk investments **after** your emergency fund is fully funded. An underfunded emergency fund is the #1 reason Indians liquidate investments at the worst time.
