# Savings Instruments in India — Where to Put Your Money

## Overview
After building an emergency fund, the next step is growing savings. India has a rich ecosystem of savings instruments ranging from completely safe government-backed options to market-linked investments.

## 1. Public Provident Fund (PPF)

### What It Is
A government-backed, long-term savings scheme with sovereign guarantee — your money is 100% safe.

### Key Features
- **Interest rate:** 7.1% per annum (compounded annually, revised quarterly by government)
- **Lock-in period:** 15 years (extendable in 5-year blocks)
- **Minimum deposit:** ₹500 per year
- **Maximum deposit:** ₹1.5 lakh per year
- **Tax benefit:** EEE — contributions are tax-deductible (Section 80C), interest is tax-free, maturity amount is tax-free
- **Partial withdrawal:** Allowed from 7th year
- **Loan against PPF:** Available from 3rd to 6th year

### Who Should Open PPF?
- Anyone with a long-term horizon (15+ years)
- Those in higher tax brackets who want guaranteed, tax-free returns
- NOT ideal for those needing liquidity

### Where to Open
Any nationalized bank (SBI, PNB, Bank of Baroda, etc.) or post office

## 2. Recurring Deposit (RD)

### What It Is
A fixed monthly deposit scheme where you deposit a fixed amount every month and earn guaranteed interest.

### Key Features
- **Interest rate:** 5.5%–7.5% (varies by bank; small finance banks offer higher)
- **Tenure:** 6 months to 10 years
- **Minimum:** ₹100/month (most banks)
- **No maximum limit**
- **Tax:** Interest is taxable as per income slab; TDS deducted if interest exceeds ₹40,000/year

### Best Use Case
Building a targeted fund — e.g., "I want ₹50,000 in 2 years for a two-wheeler down payment"

---

## 3. Fixed Deposit (FD)

### What It Is
A lump-sum deposit at a fixed interest rate for a chosen tenure. Safest savings instrument after PPF.

### Key Features
- **Interest rate:** 6.5%–9% (varies; small finance banks offer 8–9%)
- **Tenure:** 7 days to 10 years
- **Premature withdrawal:** Allowed with 0.5–1% penalty on interest
- **Senior citizen rate:** Extra 0.25%–0.5% at most banks
- **Tax:** Interest taxable; TDS deducted if interest > ₹40,000/year (₹50,000 for seniors)

### Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS)
- For those 60+ years old (or 55+ retired)
- Rate: 8.2% per annum (as of 2025, highest among government small savings)
- Max deposit: ₹30 lakh
- Tenure: 5 years

---

## 4. National Savings Certificate (NSC)

### Key Features
- **Interest rate:** 7.7% (compounded annually but paid at maturity)
- **Tenure:** 5 years
- **Max deposit:** No limit (minimum ₹1,000)
- **Tax:** Section 80C benefit on deposits; interest taxable but reinvested interest also qualifies for 80C each year
- Available at post offices

---

## 5. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)

### What It Is
A savings scheme exclusively for the girl child — among the highest interest rates in government small savings.

### Key Features
- **Interest rate:** 8.2% per annum (as of 2025)
- **Eligible:** Parents/guardians of girl child below 10 years of age
- **Deposits:** ₹250 to ₹1.5 lakh per year, for 15 years
- **Maturity:** When girl turns 21 (or on marriage after 18)
- **Tax:** EEE — fully tax-exempt at all stages
- **Partial withdrawal:** 50% after girl turns 18 (for education)

---

## Choosing the Right Instrument

| Goal | Recommended Instrument |
|---|---|
| Emergency fund (liquid) | High-interest savings account / Liquid mutual fund |
| Short-term (1–3 years) | RD or FD |
| Tax saving + long-term | PPF (lock-in 15 years) |
| Girl child education/marriage | Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana |
| Post-retirement safe income | SCSS (if 60+) |
| Higher returns (market-linked) | Mutual Funds (SIP) — see investment guide |
