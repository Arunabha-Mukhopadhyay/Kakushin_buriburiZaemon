# Debt Management — Handling Loans, EMIs, and Financial Stress

## Understanding Good Debt vs Bad Debt

### Good Debt (Creates or Preserves Asset Value)
- **Home loan:** You own an appreciating asset; interest is tax-deductible (Section 24b)
- **Education loan:** Builds earning capacity; interest deduction under Section 80E
- **Business loan (productive use):** Creates income

### Bad Debt (Depletes Wealth)
- **Credit card revolving balance:** 36–48% APR — the most expensive form of debt in India
- **Personal loans for consumption:** Financing vacations, weddings, gadgets on EMI at 14–24% interest
- **Payday loans / informal moneylenders:** Can be 100–1000% effective annual rate

---

## Debt-to-Income Ratio — The Critical Metric
**Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio** = Total monthly EMIs ÷ Monthly gross income × 100

| DTI Ratio | Status |
|---|---|
| Below 30% | Healthy — manageable debt |
| 30–40% | Caution — limit new debt |
| 40–50% | Stressed — prioritize repayment |
| Above 50% | Danger zone — immediate action needed |

**Example:** Monthly income ₹40,000, EMIs ₹18,000 → DTI = 45% → Stressed

Banks typically do not approve new loans if DTI exceeds 50%.

## Credit Card Debt — The Most Urgent to Clear

### How Credit Card Interest Works
- **Interest-free period:** 45–52 days from statement date if you pay FULL balance
- **Minimum due trap:** Paying minimum due (2–5% of outstanding) incurs interest on the ENTIRE balance from purchase date
- **APR:** 36–48% per annum (3–4% per month) — the highest retail interest rate in India

### Example of Minimum Due Trap
₹50,000 balance, 3% monthly interest, paying minimum 5% each month:
- Time to clear debt: ~8 years
- Total interest paid: ~₹48,000 (almost doubling the original amount)

### Priority: Pay credit card balance IN FULL every month. Never carry a balance.

## Debt Repayment Strategies

### 1. Avalanche Method (Mathematically Optimal)
List all debts by interest rate, highest first. Pay minimum on all; put all extra money toward highest-rate debt.

Example order for an Indian user:
1. Credit card (42% APR) — attack first
2. Personal loan (18% APR)
3. Vehicle loan (10% APR)
4. Home loan (8.5% APR) — last priority

### 2. Snowball Method (Psychologically Easier)
List all debts by outstanding amount, smallest first. Pay off smallest debt completely first to build momentum.

**When to use snowball:** If you have many small debts across multiple creditors — closing accounts gives psychological wins that keep you motivated.

## Emergency Strategies if Debt Becomes Unmanageable

### 1. Contact Your Bank BEFORE Defaulting
If you know you cannot make the next EMI:
- Call your bank's relationship manager or customer service
- Request a moratorium (temporary pause on EMI) — banks may allow 1–3 months during genuine hardship
- Request loan restructuring (extend tenure to reduce EMI)
- Being proactive preserves your credit score and avoids NPA classification

### 2. Loan Restructuring vs Settlement
- **Restructuring:** Extend tenure, reduce EMI — you still pay the full amount; credit score unaffected
- **One-Time Settlement (OTS):** Pay a lump sum less than outstanding; bank closes the loan — credit score severely damaged (marked "settled" not "closed" for 7 years)
- Always prefer restructuring over settlement if possible

### 3. RBI's SARFAESI Act — Secured Loans
For loans secured by property (home loans, LAP), banks can recover under SARFAESI Act without court order if default exceeds 90 days. Banks must send 60-day notice before action. During this 60-day window, approach the bank for resolution immediately.

## Improving Your CIBIL Score
- Pay all EMIs and credit card bills on time — 35% of score is payment history
- Keep credit card utilization below 30% of limit
- Do not apply for multiple loans simultaneously
- Maintain old credit accounts (credit history length matters)
- Check credit report annually and dispute errors immediately

**Score ranges:** 300–579 (Poor) | 580–669 (Fair) | 670–739 (Good) | 740–799 (Very Good) | 800–900 (Exceptional)
