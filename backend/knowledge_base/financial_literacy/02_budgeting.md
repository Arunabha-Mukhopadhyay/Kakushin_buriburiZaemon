# Budgeting for First-Time Earners in India — The 50-30-20 Rule Adapted

## Why Budgeting Matters
Without a budget, money disappears. Most first-time earners in India spend their entire salary by the 20th of the month and have nothing left by the end. A simple budget prevents this and creates the foundation for every other financial goal.

## The 50-30-20 Rule (India-Adapted)

### 50% — Needs (Essential Expenses)
These are non-negotiable monthly expenses:
- Rent / PG accommodation
- Food and groceries
- Commute / travel
- Utility bills (electricity, water, internet, mobile recharge)
- EMI payments on existing loans
- Insurance premiums

**India tip:** In metros like Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, rent alone often consumes 35–40% of income. If your needs exceed 50%, first look at reducing rent (share accommodation, move closer to work) before cutting food or transport.

### 30% — Wants (Discretionary Spending)
These are things you choose to spend on:
- Eating out, food delivery (Zomato, Swiggy)
- OTT subscriptions (Netflix, Hotstar, Amazon Prime)
- Shopping, clothes, gadgets
- Entertainment, outings
- Gym memberships

**India tip:** Many first-time earners reverse the 30 and 20 — they spend 30% on wants but only save 20% of nothing because needs already consumed 80%. Track wants ruthlessly.

### 20% — Savings and Investments
This 20% builds your financial future:
- **First 5%:** Emergency fund (until you hit 6 months of expenses)
- **Next 5–10%:** Term insurance premium + health insurance premium
- **Remaining 10–15%:** SIP in mutual funds / PPF / NPS

## A Simple Monthly Budget Template (₹30,000 Income)

| Category | Budget | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Rent + utilities | 35% | ₹10,500 |
| Food + groceries | 10% | ₹3,000 |
| Commute | 5% | ₹1,500 |
| Wants (eating out, OTT, etc.) | 15% | ₹4,500 |
| Emergency fund SIP | 5% | ₹1,500 |
| Term insurance | 2% | ₹600 |
| Investment SIP | 8% | ₹2,400 |
| Buffer | 20% | ₹6,000 |

## Zero-Based Budgeting — Even Better for Gig Workers
If your income is irregular (gig, freelance, seasonal), use zero-based budgeting:
1. At the start of each month, list your expected income
2. Assign every rupee to a category until Income - Assigned Amount = ₹0
3. Adjust month to month based on actual income

## Tools Available in India
- **Google Sheets / Excel** — free and effective
- **Walnut app** — tracks spending automatically from SMS
- **Money Manager** — expense tracking
- **YNAB (You Need A Budget)** — zero-based budgeting app

## The Single Most Important Rule
**Pay yourself first.** The moment your salary or payment arrives, immediately transfer the savings amount to a separate account. Budget on what is left, not what you hope to save.
